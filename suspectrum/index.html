About Suspectrum

The name is a wink at suspectrum — the uncanny way neurodivergent people spot their own. Recognizing each other is half the point, which is why the squad and compare features exist.

Suspectrum treats autism, ADHD, and their combination as natural variation, real strengths bundled with real costs in a world not built for them. A trait is disabling in one environment and a superpower in another, which is the whole point.

Three honest commitments

1. Trauma reveals, it doesn't create. Autism and ADHD are present from early development. Crisis and burnout often strip the mask and make traits visible, which is why so many people find out late. The power was always there.

2. Sliders, not a sorting hat. These powers are dimensional and shift with sleep, stress, and setting. You're a constellation, not a fixed type.

3. It affirms, it doesn't diagnose. This is a mirror, not a medical tool, and it points toward real support when that's what's needed.

The culturally-fair goal

Existing tools were normed on a narrow slice of people and misread everyone else. Autism is under-referred in ethnic minorities, ADOS ratings shift with race and gender, and Black and Hispanic kids get read as "defiant" instead of ADHD. People from marginalized communities often mask earlier and harder to meet family and community expectations, which makes a surface test read their powers as weakest. Correcting that, technically, by testing for differential item functioning, is the real work.

Prototype v0.3. Not a diagnostic instrument. Not a substitute for professional assessment.
