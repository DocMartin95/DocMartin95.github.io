# TRACI: A Simple Way to Structure Any AI Prompt

**For:** students and faculty, any subject
**Platform:** a reference you keep next to whatever AI tool you use

Most weak AI results come from weak prompts, and most weak prompts are missing structure, not effort. TRACI is a five-part checklist for building a prompt that returns what you actually need. Fill in the five parts, and a vague request turns into a specific one.

**T · Task.** What you want done. Be concrete about the action. *"Your task is to write five practice questions on the loop of Henle."*

**R · Role.** Who the AI should act as. A role sets the level and the stance. *"Act as a Socratic tutor who asks before telling."*

**A · Audience.** Who the result is for. This controls vocabulary and depth. *"The audience is a first-year student who has seen the material once."*

**C · Create.** What to produce, and in what form. Name the format. *"Produce open-ended questions, one at a time, with the model answer only after I attempt it."*

**I · Intent.** The goal, and what a good result looks like. *"The intent is to find the gaps in my understanding, not to confirm what I already know."*

## Copy-and-fill template

> **Task:** Your task is to [do what].
> **Role:** Act as [who], who [key behavior].
> **Audience:** The audience is [who], at [level].
> **Create:** Produce [format], with [any structure or constraints].
> **Intent:** The goal is [what success looks like]. [Optional: match the style of my exam or assessment.]

## Before and after

**Vague:** "Help me study the kidney."

**Built with TRACI:** "Your task is to quiz me on nephron physiology. Act as a tutor who gives hints, not answers, on a first miss. The audience is a first-year student preparing for a cumulative exam. Produce one open-ended question at a time, and after I answer, tell me what I got right, what I missed, and why. The goal is to surface what I do not yet understand."

The second prompt does more work because it carries more structure, not because it is longer. The same pattern works for a faculty member drafting an assessment, a student building a study plan, or anyone asking for feedback on a draft.

## One rule that never changes

Generating is step one. Checking is step two. AI will state wrong facts, doses, and citations with full confidence, so verify anything that matters against a trusted source before you rely on it.
